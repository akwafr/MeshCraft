# ADR-0007 — The aggregate is a port on the faceplate, and LACP fallback is a capability

## Context

Link aggregation today occupies a **separate tile** under the faceplate
(`LagPanel.tsx`): a "Link aggregation" box with a "New aggregate" button and, the rest
of the time, a sentence saying there are none. On a device without an aggregate — the
majority — it is a permanent block that teaches nothing.

Yet the contract already says the opposite of what the screen shows.
`wire/mdpv1/lag.go`:

> "A NAMED AGGREGATE of faceplate ports that behaves like one port : it has
> members, a mode, and a VLAN mapping of exactly a port's shape. […] in the
> bridge tables the bond appears AS an interface — indistinguishable from a
> physical port. Every platform surveyed treats the aggregate the same way
> (EOS's Port-ChannelN, IOS's PoN are interfaces), which is why `LAGState.VLAN`
> reuses `PortState` verbatim."

The data model treats the aggregate as a port. The interface treats it as a box. It
is the screen that is wrong.

A second gap, more concrete: **LACP fallback** — letting ports forward even when LACP
is not up, in the same VLAN or in another one. It is what prevents a server whose bond
is not yet configured from ending up with no network at all. `LAGState` does not carry
it, and not every platform can do it.

## Decision

**1. The aggregate becomes an interface type on the faceplate.** Aggregates are shown
as ports, after the physical interfaces, in their own block (`FacadeBlock`, like
"SFP+"). Selecting an aggregate **highlights its member ports** on the faceplate.

**2. A dashed "add" port.** After the existing aggregates, a dashed box with a `+`
icon. Activating it **dims the whole page** and leaves only the list of physical ports
on it, to select the members, with the aggregate's options on the side.

**3. Fallback is a capability, not a checkbox.** `LAGState` gains a `Fallback` field,
and the contract gains a `lag-fallback` gesture. A platform that does not declare it
**does not show the option** — nor does it show it greyed out: a disabled control
without explanation is worse than an absent one (UI-GUIDE §2, "do not paint a platform
limit in orange").

An **additive** contract change, hence v1, in the order imposed by `CLAUDE.md`: Go
types first (`wire/mdpv1/lag.go`), `schemas.json` regenerated by
`go run ./tools/mdpschema`, `openapi.yaml` by hand, then the vendored copy in the
drivers repository and a conformance check.

## Consequences

- **The faceplate stops being a picture of the hardware**, and that is the real price
  of this decision. An aggregate is not a socket; it exists nowhere on the casing.
  Accepted because the block is **labelled separately** and comes after the physical
  sockets, as "SFP+" already is — we add a register, we do not mix the two.
- **Range selection must not cross blocks.** It is measured on the physical order (cf.
  ADR-0005 and the comment on `FacadeBlockRows`); a ⇧-click from a physical port to an
  aggregate makes no sense and must be refused, not silently interpreted.
- **The dimming is a modal**, so it falls under `Modal` and the dismissal stack
  (`useDismissable`): Escape, outside click, focus trap. A hand-written overlay would
  reopen the seven defects that §8.1 closed.
- **Three states instead of two** for fallback: supported and active, supported and
  inactive, **not supported**. The third is not "inactive" — confusing them would
  suggest a gesture is possible where it is not.
- `LagPanel.tsx` disappears. Its diagnostic content (which members, which mode, up or
  not) must reappear in the side panel when an aggregate is selected, otherwise the
  redesign loses information.
- A connector that declares `lags` but not `lag-fallback` remains valid. The
  `Fallback` field in an intent sent to such a connector must be **refused
  explicitly**, not ignored: a silent refusal would write an aggregate different from
  the one the screen shows.

## Rejected alternatives

**Keep the tile and add fallback to it.** The cheapest, and it leaves the screen at odds
with its own data model. The tile would remain an empty block on most devices.

**Show aggregates mixed in with the physical ports**, at their place in the numbering.
Would destroy the reading of the faceplate as a picture of the hardware, which is the
thing this product protects the most (cf. the comment on `FacadeBlockRows` about
wrapping).

**A side panel instead of dimming.** The side panel is 300 px wide and already serves
something else; choosing members requires seeing the **whole** faceplate, which is
precisely what dimming frees up.

**Infer fallback support from a list of platforms hard-coded in the frontend.** That is
what `SOFT_RESET_DRIVERS` did before ADR-0005, and the reason it was removed: the
browser does not have to hold a catalogue of what connectors can do. The contract has a
mechanism for that, `Supports()`.

**A `fallback` boolean without a VLAN.** The request specifies "in the same or other
VLANs". A boolean would force us to come back to it, and a contract field is very
expensive to widen after the fact.
